We drove from Barcelona to Florence, then from Florence to Paris. We rented an Airbnb in Paris for nine days and spent three of them at Disneyland. Our son was three, almost four. We went for him, of course, but part of the trip was for my wife, too. Disneyland was already a place she had memories of.

## Getting there by car

We drove from Paris all three times. The parking I’d use again is the EFFIA garage at the Marne-la-Vallée–Chessy train station. We booked ahead each time. Our confirmations listed €21 for parking and a €2 booking fee. The best part with a three-year-old was how little walking was left between the car and the park entrance. [Find the garage on Google Maps](https://maps.app.goo.gl/CnSw5RrVrDoTydN38).

## The parade we liked best was between seasons

Our first visit was **Friday, November 6, 2026**. Halloween had wrapped up and the park was getting ready for Christmas. That sounded like an odd time to go, but my wife and I both liked the 5 p.m. parade that day best. Each float brought a different bunch of Disney characters, and part of the fun was looking down the street to see who was coming next.

We went back on **Thursday, November 12, and Friday, November 13** for the Christmas season. That parade was lovely too, especially if you wanted to see Mickey, Pinocchio and the princesses. The first one had more variety. If we went again, I’d happily pick a day between seasons just for that parade.

## The rides and the crowds around the castle

What I liked about the rides was the atmosphere. You can have a great time with a small kid without spending the whole ride upside down. The world around you is a big part of the ride, and for us that was plenty.

I did get grumpy around the castle. There were so many people dressed up and posing for pictures that I started wondering who the place was really for. I don’t have much patience for waiting for someone to get the perfect Instagram shot. A little farther from the castle, that feeling passed. There were families getting on rides and people looking around, and I could enjoy the place again. Besides, seeing what the park meant to my wife made it pretty clear the adults get to have their day there too.

## Where to wait for the night show

The night show was the same on our visits between seasons and during Christmas. I thought it was beautiful. It even made me tear up, which I wasn’t expecting. We saw it at 9 p.m., and one Friday it started at 8:30, so check the time for your day.

My pick is the fence in the central circle facing the castle. You get a straight-on view and something to lean against. I’d get there **about 40 minutes before the show** if you want that spot. Paris in November felt colder than I expected, especially once we stopped walking. Check the evening forecast and bring something warm for the wait.

If there are two adults, one can keep the spot while the other goes for food. I’d head to Frontierland for the huge turkey leg or the crispy chicken bites. Something hot to eat makes those last forty minutes much nicer. [Last Chance Cafe menu](https://media.disneylandparis.com/d4th/en-gb/images/P1RR02_last-chance-cafe.pdf).
